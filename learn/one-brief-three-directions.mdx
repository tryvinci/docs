Before you spend on a shoot, turn one brief into three finished directions. Compare them fairly and let the team pick with evidence instead of opinions.

  <Card title="Time to read" icon="clock">

## What you need

- A one page brief: product, audience and one feeling
- An image model, and image-to-video if you want motion
- Your real product photo as a locked reference

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write a tight brief">
    Name the product, the audience and one feeling. If the brief needs a paragraph to explain, it is not tight enough yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make three directions">
    Pick three distinct looks, for example minimal, lifestyle and bold. They should differ in setting, light and type, not in small details.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the product identical">
    Use the same product reference in all three, so you compare the direction and not the pack.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finish them equally">
    Take each direction to the same level of polish. A rough option next to a finished one never gets a fair hearing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick with the team">
    Show all three side by side, at the size they will be seen, and let the team choose.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Try it

Write your brief, then see how it turns into three direction prompts that all keep the product the same.

<DirectionBuilder />

<Tip>
  **Name each direction.** Give each direction a one word name. Feedback like "more loud, less home" is faster than "the second one but brighter".
</Tip>

<Warning>
  **Avoid this: falling for the first one.** The first direction you make anchors your taste. Finish all three before anyone gives an opinion.
</Warning>

export const DirectionBuilder = () => {
  const [b, setB] = useState({ product: "a daily face serum in an amber glass bottle", audience: "busy people aged 25 to 35", feeling: "calm" });
  const dirs = [
    ["MINIMAL", "soft pastel studio backdrop, one product on a plinth, lots of negative space, gentle top light"],
    ["LIFESTYLE", "a real bathroom shelf in morning light, hands reaching for the bottle, lived-in details"],
    ["BOLD", "deep dark background, hard rim light, oversized headline type, high contrast"],
  ];
  const inp = { width: "100%", padding: 8, borderRadius: 8, border: "1px solid rgba(127,127,127,.35)", background: "transparent", color: "inherit", font: "inherit" };
  return (
    <div className="not-prose" style={{ border: "1px solid rgba(127,127,127,.25)", borderRadius: 14, padding: 16, display: "grid", gap: 12 }}>
      {Object.keys(b).map((k) => (
        <label key={k} style={{ display: "grid", gap: 4, fontSize: 13, textTransform: "capitalize" }}>{k === "feeling" ? "One feeling" : k}
          <input value={b[k]} onChange={(e) => setB({ ...b, [k]: e.target.value })} style={inp} />
        </label>
      ))}
      <div style={{ display: "grid", gridTemplateColumns: "repeat(auto-fit, minmax(200px, 1fr))", gap: 10 }}>
        {dirs.map(([n, look]) => (
          <div key={n} style={{ padding: 12, borderRadius: 10, border: "1px solid rgba(127,127,127,.3)", display: "grid", gap: 8 }}>
            <div style={{ fontFamily: "ui-monospace, monospace", fontSize: 12, color: "#21dad2", letterSpacing: 1 }}>{n}</div>
            <div style={{ fontSize: 13, lineHeight: 1.45 }}>{`Ad for ${b.audience}. Product: ${b.product}, unchanged from the reference photo. Look: ${look}. Feeling: ${b.feeling}.`}</div>
          </div>
        ))}
      </div>
      <div style={{ fontSize: 13, opacity: 0.7 }}>The product line is identical in all three. Only the look changes.</div>
    </div>
  );
};
